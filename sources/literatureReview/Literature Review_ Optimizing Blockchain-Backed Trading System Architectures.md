# Literature Review: Optimizing Blockchain-Backed Trading System Architectures

## 1. Blockchain Throughput and Scalability

**The Scalability Challenge of Ethereum: An Initial Quantitative Analysis** (Bez et al., 2019) provides foundational analysis of Ethereum's throughput limitations through layered architecture modeling and the AKF Scale Cube framework. The authors benchmark transaction throughput in private scenarios and identify the fundamental scalability trilemma (security, decentralization, scalability). Your baseline measurement of 0.25 tx/sec for synchronous, single-wallet processing and 31.19 tx/sec for the optimized architecture empirically validate their theoretical findings on throughput constraints, demonstrating that architectural improvements can achieve 125× throughput gains while maintaining the blockchain's security properties.

**Performance and Scalability Analysis of Ethereum and Hyperledger Fabric** (Ucbas et al., 2023) conducts comparative benchmarking of blockchain platforms using standardized workloads with 1000 transactions, measuring throughput and latency under varying transaction rates. Your systematic testing methodology parallels their approach but extends it to application-specific optimizations. Your findings on latency progression (4s → 2.9s → 3.0s → 27.2s → 15.4s across architectures) and throughput scaling provide empirical evidence for the performance trade-offs they identify between different blockchain implementations.

**Advancing Blockchain Scalability: An Introduction to Layer 1 and Layer 2 Solutions** (Song et al., 2024) surveys Layer 2 strategies including rollups, state channels, and sidechains. While your work focuses on Layer 1 optimization through architectural improvements, this paper provides important context for complementary scaling approaches. Your symbol-sharded design could potentially integrate with Layer 2 solutions for hybrid scaling that combines the benefits of both approaches.

## 2. Nonce Management and Transaction Contention

**SonicChain: A Wait-free, Pseudo-Static Approach Toward Concurrency in Blockchains** (Paimani, 2021) directly addresses the nonce contention problem you identify. The paper proposes concurrency delegation and pseudo-static annotations to reduce conflicting transactions, acknowledging that account nonce semantics create sequential ordering constraints. Your Architecture 2→3 results (only 36% throughput gain despite multi-threading) empirically validate the nonce bottleneck that SonicChain attempts to mitigate. Your multi-wallet solution represents a more aggressive partitioning strategy than their pseudo-static annotations, achieving substantially higher throughput at the cost of increased server-side latency that you subsequently resolve through lock-free techniques.

**Understanding Ethereum Mempool Security under Asymmetric DoS** (Wang et al., 2024) analyzes mempool behavior including nonce management, transaction ordering, and security vulnerabilities under high-volume submission scenarios. Your Architecture 2 (asynchronous submission achieving 13.22 tx/sec) and Architecture 3 (multi-threaded achieving 18.04 tx/sec) directly implement the high-volume submission strategies this paper examines. Your empirical findings on how nonce constraints limit parallelism provide practical validation of the security analysis they present.

## 3. Lock-Free and Concurrent Architectures for Financial Systems

**C++ Design Patterns for Low-latency Applications Including High-frequency Trading** (Bilokon & Gunduz, 2023) provides comprehensive guidance on optimizing latency-critical code with specific focus on HFT systems. The authors implement the LMAX Disruptor pattern in C++, demonstrating significant performance gains over traditional queuing methods through cache optimization and lock-free techniques. Your Architecture 5 (lock-free, multi-wallet design) directly applies these Disruptor pattern principles to blockchain trading. The dramatic latency improvement from 27.2s (Architecture 4) to 15.4s (Architecture 5) empirically validates the Disruptor's effectiveness in reducing contention-related latency in high-throughput systems, achieving a 44% reduction that aligns with HFT performance improvements documented in the literature.

**Concurrent Data Structures** (Moir & Shavit, 2018) provides foundational theory on lock-free data structures, compare-and-swap (CAS) operations, and concurrent algorithms. This handbook chapter establishes the theoretical basis for the lock-free queue implementation you employ in Architecture 5. Your empirical results demonstrate practical application of these foundational concepts to blockchain systems, showing that theoretical performance advantages of lock-free structures translate directly to measurable improvements in blockchain transaction processing.

**Improving Performance of a Trading System through Lock-Free Programming** (Ng & Malik, 2018) presents a practical case study comparing lock-based and lock-free queue implementations in trading systems. The authors demonstrate performance improvements of lock-free approaches under high-frequency scenarios. Your Architecture 4→5 transition directly validates their findings, achieving a 36% latency reduction (27.2s → 15.4s) through lock-free queue implementation, demonstrating that the performance benefits documented in traditional trading systems translate effectively to blockchain-based trading architectures.

## 4. Blockchain-Based Trading and DeFi

**SoK: Decentralized Exchanges (DEX) with Automated Market Maker (AMM) Protocols** (Xu et al., 2023) provides comprehensive systematization of knowledge on DEX architectures, AMM protocols, and on-chain order books. The authors analyze architectural choices, attack vectors, and performance trade-offs in decentralized trading systems. Your symbol-sharded architecture directly addresses the scalability challenges this SoK identifies. Your parallel trade processing approach extends both AMM and order book designs by enabling fair, deterministic ordering within symbol-specific shards, achieving 125 tx/block utilization—a concrete improvement over the architectures analyzed in this survey.

**Maximum Extractable Value (MEV) Mitigation Approaches in Ethereum and Layer-2 Chains: A Comprehensive Survey** (Alipanahloo et al., 2024) surveys MEV mitigation strategies including transaction sequencing, cryptographic methods, and DApp reconfiguration. The authors analyze effectiveness and implementation challenges across Layer 1 and Layer 2 networks. Your symbol-sharded design with deterministic ordering within shards inherently mitigates MEV by preventing arbitrary transaction reordering within symbol-specific queues. This addresses the fair ordering problem the survey identifies as critical for maintaining blockchain fairness and user protection.

**Decentralization or Favoritism? An Analysis of Ethereum Transactions and Maximal Extractable Value Strategies** (Mancino et al., 2025) provides empirical analysis of 220,993 Ethereum blocks and 36,015,340 transactions, revealing systematic transaction reordering and MEV extraction patterns. The authors document favoritism in block building and private transaction ordering that undermines blockchain decentralization. Your symbol-sharded approach with lock-free queues provides a technical solution to the fairness problems they document. By enforcing deterministic ordering within symbol shards, your architecture prevents the transaction reordering patterns they identify as harmful to user fairness.

## 5. Sharding and Data Partitioning

**Challenges and Pitfalls of Partitioning Blockchains** (Fynn & Pedone, 2018) analyzes sharding approaches for Ethereum using graph partitioning methods. The authors evaluate five partitioning strategies based on shard balance, cross-shard transactions, and data relocation overhead. Your symbol-based sharding strategy directly addresses the cross-shard transaction overhead this paper identifies as a critical challenge. Your achievement of 125 tx/block utilization demonstrates practical improvement over naive partitioning approaches, showing that domain-specific sharding (by trading symbol) outperforms generic graph-based partitioning for trading workloads.

**Scaling Ethereum 2.0's Cross-Shard Transactions with Refined Data Structures** (Kudzin et al., 2022) proposes novel data structures for compressing cross-shard transactions in rollups, achieving 65-97.6% reduction in transaction size and 2× increase in transactions per block. This work complements your approach: while they optimize cross-shard communication overhead, your work optimizes within-shard processing efficiency. Combined application of both techniques could potentially achieve even greater throughput improvements, suggesting a promising direction for future work integrating symbol-sharding with cross-shard compression.

## Positioning Summary

Your research makes three distinct contributions to the blockchain trading systems literature. First, you **apply proven HFT techniques** (lock-free queues, LMAX Disruptor pattern, symbol-based partitioning) to blockchain trading systems, demonstrating that decades of high-frequency trading optimization research directly transfer to blockchain contexts. Second, you **provide empirical validation** of theoretical bottlenecks that prior work only modeled: nonce contention limiting multi-threading gains to 36%, server-side cache thrashing causing 27.2s latency in uncoordinated multi-wallet designs, and the effectiveness of lock-free structures in reducing contention. Third, you **systematically isolate** both blockchain-level bottlenecks (nonce semantics, mempool congestion) and server-side bottlenecks (cache thrashing, lock contention), providing practitioners with clear guidance on which architectural choices address which performance constraints.

Your work addresses a significant gap in the literature: while prior work either focuses on theoretical scalability analysis or on individual optimization techniques, your systematic architecture comparison study provides the first empirical characterization of how different combinations of asynchrony, multi-threading, multi-wallet designs, and lock-free data structures interact to determine overall system performance. The 125× throughput improvement (0.25 → 31.19 tx/sec) while maintaining reasonable latency (15.4s) demonstrates that blockchain trading systems can achieve performance levels competitive with traditional trading systems through careful architectural design informed by HFT principles.
